 A B S T R A C T
EEG signal can capture spatial and temporal shifts in electrical activity of the brain, thereby acting as a 
prominent biomarker to diagnose seizure. Nevertheless, as EEG recordings are generally high-dimensional and 
noisy, manual examination of any such abnormalities in EEG patterns to diagnose seizure is a time consuming 
and tedious task for a neurologist. Therefore, it is important to devise an effective decision support system 
which can uncover the abnormalities in the EEG signals. In this study, we present an EEG based seizure 
classification framework which leverages the potentials of variational mode decomposition (VMD) technique 
and a Bayesian optimized support vector machine (BOSVM) to classify the type of generalized motor and 
non-motor seizures. Unlike existing methods that rely on statistical or time–frequency features, this study 
exploits entropy metrics extracted from VMD-decomposed EEG modes for seizure classification. To select the 
most discriminative features, we harness both neighborhood component analysis (NCA) technique and ReliefF 
algorithm and apply feature fusion method to train the classifier models. For addressing the class imbalance 
problem in the dataset, an adaptive synthetic (ADASYN) sampling, which can augment the synthetic samples to 
minority classes, is adopted. Experiments conducted on a publicly available temple university hospital (TUH) 
dataset substantiate that the proposed framework achieves an average classification accuracy of 98.37% and 
offers improved generalization and robustness compared to the state-of-the-art machine learning classifier 
models.
1. Introduction
Epilepsy is a common chronic brain disorder that affects around 65 
million people worldwide. Epilepsy is typically characterized by peri-
odic seizures which manifest uncontrolled jerking movement, muscle 
contractions and convulsions [1]. Hence, it is crucial to diagnose the 
onset of seizure at the early stage so as to facilitate appropriate medi-
cation for epilepsy. In this regard, electroencephalogram (EEG) signals 
have been the cornerstone for seizure diagnosis for their inexpensive, 
non-invasive and long-time monitoring approach [2,3]. Since EEG can 
reflect the regular brain activity, the electrical signatures of seizures 
along with the severity, frequency and duration can be effectively 
identified using EEG. Moreover, for its excellent temporal resolution, 
EEG can capture the swift changes in brain activity, thereby assisting 
to identify the type and origin of seizures. Unlike other neuroimaging 
techniques including MRI and PET scans, EEG is not only cost effective

Typically, EEG signals exhibit seizure as spike-wave discharges for

for the stochastic nature of EEG signals, it is time-consuming and 
labor intensive for the neurologists to detect the seizure from EEG

seizure diagnosis largely depends on the experience and expertise of 
the neurologists, leading to the possibility of misdiagnosis. Therefore, 
to assist the neurologists to diagnosis the seizure and automate the 
seizure detection procedure in a clinical setting, several researchers 
have explored devising a seizure decision support system using machine 
learning algorithms [7].

1.1. Challenges in EEG based seizure classification
EEG based seizure type classification is an ongoing field of research 
due to the complexity and variability of EEG patterns related with 
various seizure types. For instance, EEG signals will typically be influ-
enced by noise or artifacts due to muscle activity and eye movements, 
obscuring the seizure patterns. Certain types of seizure, including 
generalized and focal seizures, may exhibit overlapping characteristics 
on EEG and are hard to detect and classify accurately. Moreover, 
certain seizures like absence seizure may manifest subtle differences 
in EEG which are hard to recognize even for skilled clinicians, thereby 
increasing the complexity to classify the seizure [18]. In this direction, 
by decoding the subtle differences in the EEG signals, ML can provide 
substantial support to automate the seizure diagnosis and predict the 
impact of particular anti-epileptic drugs. In this direction, a few of 
the notable ML algorithms reported on EEG based seizure diagnosis is 
critically evaluated and reported in Table  1. From EEG viewpoint, some 
of the commonly adopted techniques in the existing studies for feature 
representation are discrete wavelet transform [8,13,19], continuous

1.2. Motivation and research gap
In spite of the considerable research attention paid to EEG based 
seizure diagnosis, as reported in Table  1, the open research problems 
that need further investigation are as follows. Firstly, the EEG signals 
manifest large inter-subject variability for the differences in age, class 
of seizure and brain structure. Secondly, as the EEG signals are not 
only nonlinear but also non-stationary, the conventional decomposition 
techniques such as DWT and CWT may fail to capture the subtle 
variations in the EEG signals. In addition, EEG signals are generally 
contaminated with noise due to muscle cross-talk and motion artifacts, 
which DWT may falter to efficiently separate the true signal from noise, 
resulting in distorted feature vector and error-prone classification. As 
proven in the literature, DWT suffers from several limitations including 
fixed time–frequency resolution, sensitivity to noise and non-adaptive 
nature to the signals. Consequently, for effective feature extraction, 
EEG signals require an adaptive decomposition technique that can 
handle the nonlinearity and non-stationarity of the signals. Another 
under-explored research domain in the ML based seizure diagnosis 
is the potential of entropy features. Although the efficacy of entropy 
features to decode the other physiological signals such as ECG, EOG 
and EMG have been successfully proven in the literature, the impact 
of entropy features from EEG signals to diagnose seizure needs further 
investigation. Motivated to address the aforementioned research gaps, 
this study presents an entropy features based seizure sub-type classi-
fications by leveraging the potentials of VMD technique and optimal 
feature selection algorithms augmented with BOSVM classifier model. 
Unlike most of the existing techniques that utilized time domain and 
frequency domain features for ML training, as an alternate set of fea-
tures for EEG representation for seizure diagnosis, this study formulates 
a motor and non-motor seizure classification framework using entropy 
features from EEG signals and examines the efficacy of the proposed 
VMD augmented BOSVM framework on a publicly available Temple 
University Hospital Seizure (TUSZ) dataset.
1.3. Proposed scheme and contributions
This article presents a novel EEG based seizure classification frame-
work by combining the advantages of VMD and BOSVM techniques. 
The proposed approach effectively handles the non-linear dynamics 
of EEG signals through VMD and identifies the motor and non-motor 
seizures through BOSVM model with good accuracy. Compared with 
other signal decomposition techniques, VMD method can effectively 
deal with the modal aliasing effect and decompose the EEG signals into 
IMFs that characterize the local feature information. Moreover, VMD 
offers enhanced time–frequency representation of the time series signal, 
which is highly relevant to seizure diagnosis for identifying the spikes 
in the brain activity recordings. Extracting the robust entropy features 
from the significant IMFs, this study trains the multi-class SVM model to 
classify the seizures based on ILAE types. The hyper-parameters of the 
SVM model are optimized using the Bayesian optimization approach 
and 10-fold cross validation method is applied to address the overfitting 
problem in the classifier model. The potential of the proposed approach 
is tested on a publicly available TUSZ dataset which contains the largest 
EEG recordings of several seizure classes. Experimental results substan-
tiate the potential of the proposed VMD augmented BOSVM model to 
classify the seizures by capturing the subtle variations through entropy 
metrics. Moreover, to offer interpretability on the impact of features on 
the classifier prediction, this study adopts the explainable AI technique 
called local interpretable model-agnostic explanations (LIME), which 
focuses largely on individual predictions. In this direction, the major 
contributions of this investigation are as follows.
• We harness the potentials of VMD and BOSVM techniques to han-
dle the non-stationary characteristics of EEG signals and classify 
the type of seizure by training the classifier model using the en-
tropy features, which not only enhances the generalization ability 
of the ML classifiers but also minimizes the false predictions.
• Decomposing the EEG signals into band-limited IMFs using VMD 
technique, this study performs the spectral analysis of the IMFs 
to identify the salient modes of scalp EEG signals that contain 
significant power content. Consequently, the highly distinguish-
able IMFs are utilized to extract the prominent feature vector for 
classifier training.
• To minimize the computational burden on the classifier model, 
this study adopts NCA and ReliefF feature selection algorithms 
and fuses the prominent features to train the classifier model. 
Moreover, to minimize the class imbalance problem in the dataset 
considered for experimentation, we leverage ADASYN technique 
which can enhance the classifier sensitivity towards the minority 
class, thereby ensuring balanced view towards all possible classes.

2. Seizure overview
An abrupt and uncontrolled electrical disturbances in the brain 
cause epileptic seizures which affect the sensations, behavior and con-
sciousness of the subjects. Majorly, according to ILAE, the seizures are 
classified into three categories namely: focal, generalized and unknown 
onset. Fig.  1 illustrates the representative brain images of the three 
classes of seizures. Accurate diagnosis of type of epileptic seizure is 
paramount for appropriate medication and treatment. Focal seizures, 
which originate on one side of the brain, can further be categorized 
into simple partial seizure and complex partial seizure. Similarly, gen-
eralized seizures, which affect both sides of the brain, can be classified

To diagnose and classify the type of seizure, EEG signals play

invasive, portable, and cost effective approach. Moreover, EEG offers 
high temporal resolution and can effectively reflect the patterns in 
the brainwaves and help to decode the deviations including rhyth-

assessment is a promising seizure diagnosis technique which can guar-
antee early intervention and minimize potential cognitive dysfunction.

the protocols followed for acquiring the EEG data for seizure diagnosis.
2.1. Dataset description
This study utilizes the TUSZ v2.0.3 publicly available dataset, which 
is the largest multi-class seizure dataset, collected at Temple University 
Hospital from 2002 to 2012 [20]. TUSZ (Temple University Seizure 
Dataset) is a unique dataset that contains EEG recordings of 300 
subjects with larger than 3000 seizure events along with their labels. 
For EEG signal acquisition, international 10/20 electrode system was 
adopted. Fig.  4 illustrates the electrode system followed for recording 
the EEG signals. The electrodes were evenly located on the scalp with 
the space between electrodes varying either 10% or 20% the distance 
from front (nasion) to back (inion) [21]. In this study, we primarily 
consider the following six seizure events present in the TUSZ dataset: 
1. Focal Non-specific Seizure (FNSZ) 2. Complex Partial Seizure (CPSZ) 
3. Absence Seizure (ABSZ) 4. Generalized Non-specific Seizure (GNSZ) 
5. Tonic Seizure (TNSZ) and 6. Tonic-clonic Seizure (TCSZ). The demo-
graphic details of the subjects participated in the data acquisition are 
presented in Table  2. In this investigation, we majorly target to classify 
the seizure type by exploiting the hidden features through the adaptive 
decomposition and spectral analysis. TUSZ dataset contains the scalp 
EEG recordings collected at different sampling rates varying from 250 
to 512 Hz. The data were acquired according to the international 10–20 
electrode system. To decode the brain function, TUSZ utilized two 
unipolar montages namely average reference (AR) and linked ears ref-
erence (LE) as shown in Fig.  4. While AR method offers a balanced view 
of the brain function by minimizing the reference-specific artifacts, LE 
provides the precision needed to detect focal abnormalities.
The raw EEG signals of the six seizure classes are illustrated in Table 
3. As the EEG signals manifest high nonlinearity and non-stationary 
dynamics, it is crucial to preprocess the signals before extracting the 
features for classifier training. Hence, we adopt initial preprocessing 
steps including data normalization and filtering. To remove high fre-
quency noise and DC drift caused by muscle artifacts, a bandpass filter 
(1–40 Hz) is implemented. Moreover, the signals are normalized using 
Z-score normalization technique so as to mitigate the impact of baseline 
drift and spikes.

3.2. VMD for signal denoising and decomposition
VMD is an adaptive signal decomposition technique which is highly 
suitable for nonlinear and non-stationary signals. As the EEG signals 
manifest non-linear and non-stationary dynamics, this study adopts 
VMD technique to decompose the brain activity signal into the so 
called intrinsic mode functions (IMFs) or a group of band-limited 
modes [22]. Each IMF is characterized by an amplitude-modulated 
frequency-modulated (AM-FM) signal. Leveraging variational optimiza-
tion technique to solve the constrained optimization problem, VMD 
computes the band limited modes automatically around a central fre-
quency and decomposes the signal into IMFs [23]. Through a La-
grangian multiplier, VMD introduces the constraint that the aggregate 
of the IMFs should result in the original signal. Hence, VMD is used 
not only for decomposing the signal but also for denoising the high 
frequency contents from the original signal. The key benefit of VMD 
over other decomposition techniques such as EMD is that VMD elimi-
nates the mode mixing issue and is robust against noise, thus offering 
computationally efficient approach for real-time signal processing. To 
minimize the optimization task, VMD operates in frequency domain 
and updates the modes iteratively by adjusting the frequency spectrum 
of the signal based on the center frequency. Hence, through iterative 
updates of modes, the Lagrange multiplier and center frequencies, the 
VMD technique terminates when the frequencies and modes reach 
constant values.
Solving the constrained variational problem, VMD decomposes the 
EEG signal into a group of band-limited IMFs and effectively separates 
the noise components from the EEG signals. Moreover, VMD miti-
gates the impact of mode mixing problem which is common in other

3.3. Feature extraction
This study adopts the entropy features to train the ML classifier 
model for seizure prediction. Entropy features can offer valuable in-
sights into the nature and dynamics of EEG signals, thereby help-
ing to distinguish the different states of seizure. For instance, the 
dispersion entropy can detect the subtle changes in the EEG signal, 
which conventional statistical measures may fail to discover. More-
over, statistical measures are typically centered on spread and central 
tendencies, which may fail to represent the underlying non-linear and 
non-stationary characteristics of the brain signals [24]. Unlike statisti-
cal metrics that are largely susceptible to noise, entropy features are 
highly robust against noise because they ignore irrelevant variations 
caused by noise and capture the cardinal structure of the data [25]. 
Compared to conventional features, entropy features can manifest pre-
cise summary of signals complexity without losing vital information, 
thereby offering dimensionality reduction. It is also proven in the 
literature that augmenting entropy features in the ML pipelines not only 
enhances the generalization ability but also minimizes the false predic-
tions. This study extracts eight entropy measures given in Table  4 to 
capture the hidden features of the seizure classes. Table  4 presents the 
importance of the entropy measures computed from the decomposed 
EEG signals and their respective mathematical expressions.

3.4.2. ReliefF-feature selection technique
ReliefF is a filter based feature selection method that is widely 
used in supervised learning tasks with noisy and high dimensional 
dataset. Harnessing the neighborhood information to extract the key 
features which are best suitable to differentiate the classes, ReliefF can 
reduce the influence of redundant features by allocating low weights 
to them [29,30]. Moreover, as ReliefF is a non-parametric method that 
does not require any particular distribution of data, it can be used to 
handle imbalance in class distribution. The goal of ReliefF algorithm 
is to compute the weights of predictors iteratively by assigning higher 
weights to features which result in larger separation between different 
classes.
3.5. Class imbalance — ADASYN
To deal with the imbalance seizure dataset, this study leverages 
an adaptive synthetic (ADASYN) sampling approach, which is a class 
of oversampling technique. As a variant of Synthetic minority over-
sampling technique (SMOTE), ADASYN generates synthetic samples 
according to the level of difficulty in discerning the minority sam-
ples [31]. Unlike SMOTE that focuses on generating the synthetic 
samples evenly across the minority class, ADASYN aims to generate 
synthetic samples where the class samples are hard to classify. Con-
sequently, ADASYN realizes a refined decision boundary and enhances 
the sensitivity for the minority class. Moreover, by assisting the models 
to learn from challenging samples through dynamic weighting strategy, 
ADASYN can significantly improve the generalization ability of the 
classifier model on unseen data [32]. The core idea in ADASYN is 
to utilize a weighted distribution for minority class samples based 
on their difficulty level in learning and generate artificial samples 
of the minority classes to mitigate overfitting to particular samples. 
Consequently, the ADASYN technique helps to enhance the classifier 
sensitivity towards the minority class, thereby ensuring balanced view 
towards all possible classes. Algorithm 2 presents the pseudo code 
of ADASYN technique to handle the class imbalance problem in the 
seizure classification.

3.7. Bayesian optimization
The primary objective is to automate the task of computing the 
optimal SVM hyper-parameters through a probabilistic model of the 
cost function. Typically, utilizing a surrogate model or a Gaussian pro-
cess, Bayesian optimization iteratively determines the optimal hyper-
parameters, striking a balance between exploitation and exploration. 
Unlike the conventional techniques such as grid search and random 
search, Bayesian optimization offers numerous benefits while tuning 
classifier models, including higher sample efficiency, more flexibil-
ity in initializing search space and more robust optimization in the 
presence of noise. Moreover, Bayesian approach aids in computing 
the optimal values and effectively avoids the local optima. Another 
interesting feature of BO is that it requires minimal function evaluations 
to compute the optimal parameters, thereby enabling efficient and 
systematic optimization in case of high-dimensional domain. The major 
steps of BO are as follows. Firstly, the range of the hyper-parameters 
(𝐶 and 𝛾) and the objective functions are defined. Secondly, through 
a probabilistic model, the objective functions are evaluated according 
to the outcome the surrogate model are updated iteratively. Finally, 
using the acquisition function, the next set of hyper-parameters are 
evaluated and this process is repeated until the stopping criterion 
(specified iterations or marginal improvement) is met. Algorithm 3 
presents the steps involved in the Bayesian optimization to optimize

4. Results and discussion
The proposed VMD augmented BOSVM classifier model for seizure 
classification is implemented in MATLAB 2024. Analyzing the raw 
data in TUSZ reveals that the EEG recordings are not standardized 
and have unequal data samples for different seizure classes. Hence, 
firstly, we perform pre-processing of EEG signals before extracting the 
prominent feature vectors for classifier training. Specifically, as the 
frequencies less than 40 Hz are crucial for diagnosing the seizures, 
the raw EEG signals are filtered using a low-pass filter with a cut-off 
frequency of 50 Hz. Then, through windowing technique, the EEG seg-
ments that contain the abnormal neural activity, depicting the seizure 
classes, are extracted from the raw data. The data samples available 
for the FNSZ and GNSZ classes are 10,046 s and 8774 s, respectively. 
Fig. 6. Exemplary EEG signal decomposition using VMD technique.
Similarly, the EEG recordings present for other classes such as TNSZ, 
TCSZ, ABSZ and CPSZ are 5032 s, 3396 s, 2098 s, and 1256 s, re-
spectively. The aforementioned data samples clearly reveal that the 
dataset is highly imbalanced and requires effective technique to handle 
class-imbalance problem in classification. Hence, this study adopts an 
ADASYN technique to mitigate the sensitivity of the classes that have 
higher samples. After pre-processing and segmenting the EEG signals, 
we implement a VMD technique majorly to denoise the signals and to 
decompose the brain signals into seizure-related frequency components 
in the form of IMFs. Each IMF depicts a particular frequency band and 
the spectral analysis of IMFs helps to uncover high-energy spikes in 
specific frequency bands, representing seizure event. Fig.  6 illustrates 
the representative five level decomposition of the EEG signals using 
VMD technique. By eliminating noisy or nonessential frequency modes, 
VMD can effectively reconstruct the refined version of the EEG signals. 
Fig.  7 depicts the raw EEG signal and the cleaner version of the same 
reconstructed using the VMD technique. Hence, this study adopts VMD 
for two primary roles namely denoising and decomposing the EEG 
signals. After decomposing the brain signals into specific frequency 
bands, we perform the spectral evaluation of the decomposed IMFs to 
minimize the computational burden on the classifier model. Through 
spectral analysis, this study uncovers the IMFs that have significant 
power content. Fig.  8 illustrates the spectral plot of the IMFs for the 
six seizure classes considered for study. The dominant IMFs of each 
of the seizure classes are utilized to compute the entropy features for

4.2. Ablation experiments
To assess the role of VMD technique and Bayesian optimization on 
the seizure classification problem, we perform ablation experiments. 
From the performance metrics for the ablation experiments given in 
Table  9, we can note that augmenting VMD technique for signal de-
composition and denoising improves the classifier accuracy by 5.71% 
and F1 score by 17%. Similarly, adopting Bayesian optimization with 
the classifier to tune the SVM hyperparameters increases the classifier 
accuracy by 1.7% and F1 score by 6%. It is important to note from 
these results that even though augmenting BO technique and VMD with 
the classifier model increases the computational burden, the classifier 
accuracy and generalization capability, reflected through F1 score, are 
considerably improved. The primary reason for the enhanced classifier 
performance can be attributed to the adaptive signal decomposition 
(VMD) and optimal hyperparameter selection using BO technique.

4.5. Discussion
EEG signals acquired along the scalp manifests the spontaneous 
electrical activity of the brain. Hence, EEG has been a primary tool for 
diagnosing several neuro-disorders including seizure. Manual analysis 
of EEGs for diagnosing seizure is typically time consuming because 
the recordings will generally last for hours. Hence, this study has 
presented a ML based seizure classification framework based on the 
EEG signals. To deal with the non-stationary dynamics of EEG, this 
study has adopted a VMD technique to decompose the neural signal into 
IMFs and implemented a spectral analysis of decomposed modes for 
extracting the prominent IMFs for feature extraction. Unlike most of the 
current techniques that adopted time-, frequency- and time–frequency 
features for classifier training, we explored the entropy features that 
remain largely unexplored. The findings of our investigation substanti-
ate that entropy features can act as a robust feature vector to classify 
the stages of seizure. Moreover, to identify the optimal entropy fea-
tures for classifier training, a feature fusion approach which combines 
the prominent features obtained using NCA and ReliefF algorithms is 
leveraged. Experimental results corroborate that the proposed BOSVM 
classifier model offers an average seizure classification accuracy of

4.6. Limitations of the proposed framework
Although the proposed seizure classification framework offers sig-
nificant insights into the potentials of entropy features to effectively 
categorize the seizure using BOSVM model, it is important to discuss 
the limitations of the this investigation. Firstly, extracting influential 
features from EEG signals typically require the domain expertise and 
considerable time and effort. Secondly, the data considered in this study 
were acquired in a controlled setting; consequently, the efficacy of the 
proposed approach to handle diverse noise levels in real-time setting 
needs to be further evaluated. Moreover, when EEG signals are acquired 
at different sampling rate and signal quality, the model needs to be 
retrained to ensure the generalizability of the classifier.
5. Conclusion
This study has presented an EEG based seizure classification frame-
work by leveraging the potentials of VMD and BOSVM model. The 
nonlinear and non-stationary dynamics of EEG is handled through a 
VMD technique such that the neuro signals are decomposed into band-
limited frequency modes for extracting the feature vector. By adopting 
the spectral evaluation of the decomposed EEG modes, this study has 
identified the prominent IMFs that can be utilized for entropy feature 
extraction. With the primary goal of demonstrating the potential of 
entropy features to categorize the seizures, we have harnessed the 
Bayesian optimized SVM classifier model and trained the classifier 
based on the optimal entropy features identified using the NCA and Re-
liefF algorithms. Subsequently, an ADASYN technique is implemented 
to handle the class imbalance problem such that the sensitivity of the 
classifier towards the major sample class is mitigated. The experimental 
validation on the publicly available seizure dataset substantiates that 
the proposed approach can achieve an average classification accuracy 
of 98.37% and provide higher generalization feature compared to the 
state-of-the-art classifier models. Exploiting the benefits of entropy 
features and feature fusion method, the proposed BOSVM classifier 
framework sets a foundation for accurate and interpretable seizure 
classification systems in clinical environments. While the classification 
results of the proposed framework are promising, future research can 
further evaluate the potential of multi-modal fusion technique that 
fuses the neural recording from different sources including EEG and 
MRI. Testing the performance of the proposed approach on more 
diverse dataset and expanding the feature analysis will help to improve 
the generalization ability of the classifier model. Moreover, to minimize 
the computational burden on the classifier models, the optimal EEG 
channels that contain the prominent information for predicting the 
seizure can be explored.
